Simplifying legal text is useful only if the simplified sentence keeps the legal meaning of the original. JUDGEBERT recently framed this as Legal Meaning Preservation (LMP) for French insurance clauses. We extend LMP to English and Hindi and to a new domain, tenancy law. We build EnHi-LMP, 320 parallel English--Hindi original--simplified pairs with typed legal errors and 320 sanity pairs; LegalDiff, 19 bilingual legal-difference features with a rule-based explainer; and ML-JudgeBERT, a JUDGEBERT-style cross-encoder regressor. Across five experiments, generic similarity correlates weakly with LMP ($r\le0.37$) and degrades on Hindi, while LegalDiff-based models reach $r=0.60$, transfer across languages and domains with small losses, score $0.56$--$0.59$ zero-shot on human-labelled FrJUDGE, pass identical/unrelated sanity checks and detect legal errors with F1 $0.79$. A cross-encoder trained from scratch is weak alone ($r=0.12$), motivating pretrained multilingual backbones. Labels in this version are rubric-based silver labels pending double human annotation.
